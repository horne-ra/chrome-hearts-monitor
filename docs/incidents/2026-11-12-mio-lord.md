# MIO LORD missed alert: November 12, 2026

Investigated November 13. Product: `21978827FE50010`,
[MIO LORD](https://www.chromehearts.com/eyewear/mio-lord/21978827FE50010.html).
The supplied Discord message was timestamped 12:13 PM Pacific on November 12;
that is an observation time, not independent proof of the release time.

## Findings

- Railway CLI logs show failed sweeps at 20:10:59 and 20:15:59 UTC
  (12:10:59 and 12:15:59 PM Pacific), surrounding the supplied message.
  Both received HTTP 403 from `/sitemap_index.xml`. The worker stopped the
  crawl before category requests and retried after 300 seconds. The failure
  counts were 1,657 and 1,658, respectively.
- On November 13, the persistent product history contained 57 PIDs and did
  not contain MIO LORD. Its modification time was November 6 at
  22:29:24 UTC (2:29:24 PM Pacific). The health tracker recorded 1,938
  consecutive failures and an already-delivered incident warning.
- A direct `/eyewear` request from the production container also returned
  HTTP 403, with `Server: cloudflare`, title `Attention Required! | Cloudflare`,
  and heading `Sorry, you have been blocked`. One diagnostic response had
  Cloudflare Ray ID `a5a0458cad0499f9-IAD`. Skipping sitemap discovery alone
  would not resolve this observed category block.
- From the developer machine, `/eyewear` returned HTTP 200 and redirected
  to MIO LORD. The existing parser extracted its PID, name, URL and $1,470
  price. `eyewear` was already in the monitored category list.
- Production was running the expected private-notification-routing release.
  Railway's successful deployment/process status did not establish that the
  storefront could be fetched.

The confirmed failure was storefront access from production, before discovery
and notification delivery. There is no evidence that a missing eyewear category
or Discord delivery error caused this miss. The Cloudflare response does not
identify the exact firewall rule; an IP/ASN rule, rate-based policy, or another
storefront security change remains possible. A general Railway status incident
alone cannot establish the cause.

## Contributing alert behavior and changes

The previous health policy deliberately sent one warning per incident and
suppressed all later warnings until recovery. A week-long incident therefore
remained silent after its first warning.

The follow-up change adds private outage reminders every six hours by default,
persists their delivery time across restarts, reports the product-history
update age, and explicitly warns that product alerts may be missing. Startup
notices now require a successful first sweep. The known Cloudflare block title
is recognized in diagnostics. Regression coverage reproduces discovery and
notification of MIO LORD through the eyewear redirect. Product history and
the separate product-drop destination are preserved.

## Remaining recovery work

The alert changes do not remove the storefront's HTTP 403 block. Resolve access
with the storefront/operator or hosting provider using the response time,
requested URL and Cloudflare Ray ID. Confirm permission and an allowed access
method or feed. If considering a hosting/network change, validate category and
full-catalog access before cutover and transfer the existing persistent product
history; do not seed a new history or treat a green process status as recovery.
Do not run two notifying workers against independent histories.

Recovery requires successful category requests and a complete crawl from the
actual production runtime. Only then can the monitor resume reliable detection
and send its recovery notice. Items that disappear during an outage may not be
recoverable from later crawls.
